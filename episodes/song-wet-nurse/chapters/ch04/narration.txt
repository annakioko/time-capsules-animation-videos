But the physical restrictions are nothing compared to the psychological warfare. Look out the lattice window of the nursery. See those women walking in the courtyard? That is the Empress and her entourage. She has no sons. Her womb is empty, and her biological clock is ticking against her. Every time she looks toward your nursery, your stomach twists into knots.

Back to the bowl in your hands. The concubine is hovering over you, her eyes wild with postpartum paranoia. She swears she saw a kitchen eunuch whispering to one of the Empress's maids near the well this morning.

"Drink," she whispers to you. Her voice is trembling, but her hand is on the hilt of a guard's dagger.

You bring the cool porcelain to your lips. You know that if the Empress did bribe the kitchen staff, they likely used arsenic or crushed lead. It won't taste like anything. You swallow.

One second passes. Two. Your heart thumps against your ribs. You wait for the burning in your throat. You wait for your stomach to cramp.

Nothing happens. Your lungs expand as you take a shaky breath. It's safe. For today.

The concubine snatches the bowl from your hands, her face instantly shifting from terror to cold, aristocratic indifference. She cradles her son and feeds him, completely forgetting you are even in the room. You step back into the shadows of the silk curtains, wiping your mouth. You survived the morning. But the prince has to eat again in four hours.
