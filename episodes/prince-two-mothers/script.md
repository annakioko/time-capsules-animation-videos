## History notes
- Real (checked): Kaifeng was the Northern Song capital from 960, south of the Yellow River, in today's Henan province. Renzong (Zhao Zhen) was born in 1010 to Lady Li, a maid of Consort Liu; Liu claimed him as her son; he became emperor in 1022 aged 12 (Chinese count) with Liu as regent; Lady Li kept silent and died in 1032; he learned the truth after Liu died in 1033, wept at Li's coffin and made her Empress Dowager posthumously; he reigned about 41 years, the longest of any Song emperor. Sources: Wikipedia (Consort Li, Empress Liu, Emperor Renzong, Kaifeng), Britannica (Kaifeng), Cambridge History of China.
- Story (dramatised): our prince, his nurse, the food taster, the Empress's garden scene. Chapter 7 presents Renzong as a real case like ours; in real life the roles were reversed (the maid was the birth mother).

## Script (as approved)


## Chapter 1: The taster
[ON SCREEN: dawn in the prince's bedchamber; a small boy in a jade-and-gold robe sits on a big bed; a eunuch lifts a bowl to his own lips first]

You are 9 years old, a prince in Kaifeng, and before you eat a single bite, a servant tastes it in case it kills you. But poison is not what will break you.

[ON SCREEN: title card "Kaifeng · Song dynasty China / 969 AD"; a simple map of China with Kaifeng marked just south of the Yellow River]

It is 969 AD. You live in Kaifeng, the capital of the Song Empire, on the plains of central China, just south of the Yellow River. Today it is a city in China's Henan province. The Song dynasty is only nine years old. The same age as you.

[ON SCREEN: the eunuch swallows; everyone in the room watches his face; he nods]

Every morning starts the same way. The eunuch lifts your rice porridge to his own lips, swallows, and waits. Everyone in the room watches his face. If he turns pale, the cook dies. If he smiles, you eat.

[ON SCREEN: the prince eats slowly; the wet nurse, older now, kneels by the door]

You eat slowly. Because the woman kneeling by the door taught you to. "Small bites, my prince. If it tastes bitter, stop."

She is the same woman who tasted the milk nine years ago. The palace calls her your nurse. You call her something else, but only when no one is listening.

## Chapter 2: Two mothers
[ON SCREEN: a grand hall; the consort, the prince's birth mother, sits on a raised seat in pink silk and gold hairpins]

At sunrise, you are taken to greet your mother. Your real mother. The consort who gave birth to you, and who has climbed the ranks of the palace because you lived.

[ON SCREEN: the prince kneels and touches his forehead to the floor; the consort barely looks up]

You kneel. You touch your forehead to the floor. She asks about your lessons, your health, your manners. She does not hug you. Princes are not hugged in public.

[ON SCREEN: close on the consort's face, proud and calculating; a tiny crown appears above the prince's head as a thought bubble]

To her, you are not just a son. You are her future. If you become Emperor one day, she becomes the most powerful woman in the empire. If you fail, she is nothing.

[ON SCREEN: the wet nurse waiting outside the hall, peeking through the door]

Outside the hall, the nurse waits. She is not allowed inside. She is a servant.

## Chapter 3: The lesson
[ON SCREEN: a study room with scrolls, ink stones and brushes; a stern tutor in a black scholar's cap]

Then come the lessons. Your tutor is one of the most respected scholars in the empire, and he is not here to be kind.

[ON SCREEN: the prince copies characters with a brush; the tutor taps the paper]

You copy the classics, character by character. You memorise the words of Confucius. You learn history, morality, how a ruler should think. One wrong stroke and you start again.

[ON SCREEN: the tutor writing in a small notebook while the prince isn't looking]

But your tutor is not only teaching you. He is watching you. How clever you are. How patient. How you react when you are wrong. Everything he sees can reach the Emperor.

[ON SCREEN: the prince's hand trembles over the paper, like the nurse's hands in episode one]

So you never show you are tired. You never show you are afraid. You are nine, and you are already performing.

## Chapter 4: The Empress's garden
[ON SCREEN: a bright palace garden; plum blossoms, a pond with koi; the Empress in imperial yellow and a phoenix crown]

In the afternoon, a eunuch brings a message. The Empress wishes to see you in her garden.

[ON SCREEN: the Empress smiles warmly and holds out a small lacquer box of sweets]

She is the woman your nurse taught you to fear. The woman who, nine years ago, may have tried to poison your milk. Today she smiles. She calls you a fine young prince. She offers you a sweet from a lacquered box.

[ON SCREEN: split screen: the sweet in the Empress's hand; the nurse's face in memory, saying "If it tastes bitter, stop"]

Your hand reaches out. Then you remember the voice at the door every morning.

You bow deeply. You thank her. You say your tutor forbids sweets before evening lessons. You do not take it.

[ON SCREEN: the Empress's smile stays, but her eyes narrow]

She smiles wider. And in that smile, you understand something no tutor ever taught you. In the palace, kindness is a weapon too.

## Chapter 5: The walls
[ON SCREEN: the prince climbs a stack of garden stones to peek over a tall red wall; the rooftops of the city glow beyond]

You have never walked through the city outside these walls. You have heard it: the drums, the markets, the carts, the shouting. Some evenings you climb the garden stones to look at the rooftops of Kaifeng, glowing in the sunset.

[ON SCREEN: the nurse pulls him down gently by the sleeve]

A hand pulls you down. Your nurse. "If a guard sees you, I will be beaten, not you."

[ON SCREEN: the two sit side by side against the wall; a flashback bubble shows a muddy village and a crying baby]

You sit together against the wall, and you ask her the question you always ask. What is it like out there?

She tells you about mud and rice fields and a little house by a willow tree. Then she stops. You know why. Somewhere out there is the baby she left behind to feed you. Her child would be nine now too. She has never seen them again.

## Chapter 6: The secret
[ON SCREEN: night; the prince's bedchamber lit by one candle; the nurse tucks him in]

At night, she is the one who stays. She checks the windows. She tastes your water. She sits by your bed until your eyes close.

[ON SCREEN: close on the prince's face, half asleep]

And tonight, half asleep, you whisper the word you are never allowed to say out loud.

"Goodnight, mother."

[ON SCREEN: the nurse freezes; a tear runs down her cheek; she glances at the door]

She freezes. Then she presses her hand over your mouth, gently, and looks at the door. Because if the consort ever hears you call a servant "mother", the nurse will be sent away. Or worse.

[ON SCREEN: the nurse kisses his forehead and blows out the candle]

She kisses your forehead. She says nothing. But she does not stop crying until the candle goes out.

## Chapter 7: Who raised the emperor
[ON SCREEN: the flat-style picture fades into a scroll map of Song China; the year 1010 appears]

This prince is our story. But what happened to him really did happen to a prince of the Song dynasty.

[ON SCREEN: a palace maid holding a newborn; a consort in rich robes takes the baby from her arms]

In 1010, a palace maid named Lady Li gave birth to the Emperor's son. But the baby was claimed by her mistress, the powerful Consort Liu, who raised him as her own. The boy grew up believing Liu was his mother.

[ON SCREEN: the boy, now 12, crowned; Liu behind a screen, ruling for him]

At 12 he became Emperor Renzong, and Liu ruled for him as regent. His real mother lived in the same palace, and she kept her silence.

[ON SCREEN: a coffin draped in white; the young emperor kneeling in tears]

Only after Liu died in 1033 did he learn the truth. His birth mother had already died the year before. He wept at her coffin and gave her the title she never had in life: Empress Dowager.

[ON SCREEN: the emperor on his throne, older; text "41 years on the throne"]

He went on to rule for 41 years, the longest of any Song emperor. And for most of his life, the woman who gave birth to him was a stranger in his own palace.

[ON SCREEN: back to our prince asleep, the nurse at his bedside in the dark]

The history books remember emperors and empresses. They rarely remember the maids, the nurses and the mothers who raised them in the shadows.

So tonight, ask yourself: who really raised the emperor?

[ON SCREEN: end card: "A day in the life of… The Prince With Two Mothers"; fade to black]
