This prince is our story. But what happened to him really did happen to a prince of the Song dynasty.

In ten ten, a palace maid named Lady Li gave birth to the Emperor's son. But the baby was claimed by her mistress, the powerful Consort Liu, who raised him as her own. The boy grew up believing Liu was his mother.

At twelve he became Emperor Renzong, and Liu ruled for him as regent. His real mother lived in the same palace, and she kept her silence.

Only after Liu died in ten thirty-three did he learn the truth. His birth mother had already died the year before. He wept at her coffin and gave her the title she never had in life: Empress Dowager.

He went on to rule for forty-one years, the longest of any Song emperor. And for most of his life, the woman who gave birth to him was a stranger in his own palace.

The history books remember emperors and empresses. They rarely remember the maids, the nurses and the mothers who raised them in the shadows.

So tonight, ask yourself: who really raised the emperor?
